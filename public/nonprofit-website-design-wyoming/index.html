What community groups need from a website

The best club and nonprofit sites keep it simple and keep it current:

  • When and where you meet, right on the home page, with a map and parking notes
  • An events calendar for fundraisers, service days, services, and community events
  • Online donations through a secure, dedicated donation page (HTTPS) that also works on phones
  • Volunteer sign-ups for each event or shift, so nobody has to manage a paper list
  • Member pages for rosters, newsletters, and committee documents, private if needed
  • Sponsor and partner recognition that you can update every year
  • Photo galleries that show your work in the community
  • Easy updates by volunteers, with no web developer needed for a date change

For 501(c)(3) charities, a solid website can also unlock Google Ad Grants, which provides up to $10,000 USD per month in free Google Search ads to qualifying nonprofits. To qualify, you need a high-quality website on a domain you own, served over HTTPS, with real content about your mission and no AdSense ads. Government entities, hospitals, and schools are not eligible (Google Ad Grants FAQ; Google website policy). We build sites with those requirements in mind. Google decides who gets approved.

Easy to update after handoff

Club officers change every year or two. Your website should survive the handoff.

  • One-page cheat sheet for adding an event, posting a newsletter, and changing meeting info
  • Volunteer-friendly editor: if you can write an email, you can update the site
  • Roles and logins: the secretary edits events, the treasurer manages the donate page, and the president can do it all
  • Owned by the organization: the domain, hosting, and donation accounts stay in your group's name, not a former officer's personal email
  • A handoff checklist for incoming officers: logins, renewal dates, and who to call
  • Help when you want it: we can make updates for you or check in once a season

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

We're a small volunteer group. Do we really need a website?

If people ask "when do you meet?" or "how can I help?" more than once a month, a simple website saves a lot of volunteer time. It is also the one place you control. Social media posts get buried, but your website stays put.

Can people donate on our website?

Yes. We connect a secure donate page to the giving or payment tool you choose and test it on phones. The money goes straight to your organization's account, not through us.

Can you help us apply for Google Ad Grants?

We build the website to meet Google's published Ad Grants website requirements: a domain you own, HTTPS, real mission content, and working donation links. Your organization applies through Google for Nonprofits, and Google makes the decision. Ad Grants is for eligible charitable nonprofits, not government entities, hospitals, or schools.

Can our secretary update events without calling you?

Yes. Adding an event takes a short form: title, date, time, place, and a sentence or two. We train whoever will do it and leave a written guide for the next officer.

Do you build church websites?

Yes. Churches usually need service times, a plain "Plan your visit" page, an events calendar, ministry pages, and online giving. We keep the design welcoming and simple to update each week.

Can we use our national club's logo?

Most national and international organizations publish rules for how local clubs use their name and emblem. We follow your organization's rules and use the official files you provide. We won't recreate or alter an official logo.
