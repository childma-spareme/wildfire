Local intro: peak-season phones, Maps, and “near me”

In peak summer and winter, Jackson’s search traffic looks different from most Wyoming towns. Visitors already in town — or planning the trip the week before — search for tours, guides, rentals, repairs, and “near me” services on a phone. They are not reading long brand stories. They want to know what you offer, whether you are open, and how to call or book in a few taps.

That means your website and Google Business Profile have to work together:

  • Show up on Google Maps when someone searches “wildlife tour Jackson Hole,” “rafting Jackson WY,” “auto repair near me,” or “ski rental Jackson”
  • Make the phone number and booking or quote path obvious on mobile
  • Match what your listing says (hours, services, photos) so a tourist who finds you on Maps lands on a page that converts
  • Stay useful in the shoulder seasons, when locals and planners still search but the rush slows down

A national template with “Jackson” dropped into filler copy does not answer those searches. Neither does a pretty page with a buried phone number.

Peak season, shoulder season, and booking flow

Peak summer and winter bring phone-heavy, last-minute, and “near me” searches. Shoulder seasons bring more planning searches from people booking ahead and from year-round locals. Your pages should cover both:

  • Trip or service pages that name what you actually run (tours, repairs, rentals, lodging support)
  • Obvious Call and Book / Request a quote buttons above the fold on mobile
  • Hours, season dates, and “what to expect” so visitors are not guessing
  • Google posts and photos that refresh when the season changes instead of going quiet for months

We plan that into the build so you are not stuck with a summer-only homepage in January — or a winter brochure when rafting inquiries start.
