Why your phone isn't ringing from Google

You show up somewhere on Google. Maybe you even get a few profile views. The phone still sits quiet. That gap is where most Wyoming owner-operators lose work — and it is usually fixable.

Your Google listing is incomplete or in the wrong category

Being “on Google” is not the same as being easy to choose. Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees in the map pack. If the primary category is vague, the services list is empty, the hours are wrong, or the photos are old, people keep scrolling. Completeness matters: name, categories, services you actually offer, service areas you actually cover, photos of real work, and a website button that goes to a page that can take a call.

Category is not a small detail. A remodeling company listed only as a generic construction company is competing in a wider, noisier bucket than it needs to. Pick the category that matches the work you want the phone to ring for, then fill in the rest of the profile so it agrees with your website. For the local search side of that, see local SEO in Wyoming and, if Riverton is your home market, local SEO in Riverton.

Reviews went quiet, or the site is hard to call

Reviews and recency

In a small Wyoming market, a handful of recent Google reviews can decide who gets the tap. An older rating with no new comments looks like a business that may not be taking work. Ask happy customers when the job is done, give them a direct link, and reply to what you get. No fake reviews, and no bought ones. We wrote up that habit on our Google reviews page.

A missing tap-to-call button

Most of these searches happen on a phone. If your number is an image, buried in the footer, or stuck in a PDF, a lot of busy customers will bounce. A clear tap-to-call button near the top of the page is one of the simplest fixes in lead generation. If you want the longer version, read stop hiding your phone number.

Slow or unclear mobile pages

The page can be “fine on a computer” and still fail in a driveway. If it loads slowly, jumps around, or hides what you do behind a long brand story, the customer leaves before they ever see the number. We design Wyoming websites so a phone visitor can tell what you do, where you work, and how to call without pinching and scrolling for it.

No service or town pages, and listings that do not match

No service or town pages

Showing up for your business name is not the same as showing up for the work people are hiring. If the site never names the services you sell, or the towns you cover, Google and customers both have to guess. That is why we build town and service pages for places we actually work, including Fremont County, Riverton, Lander, and the smaller towns along the corridor such as Dubois, Shoshoni, and Hudson. We do not invent a local office we do not have. We do name the places you truly serve.

Name, address, and phone that do not match

Your name, city, and phone should be the same on your website, your Google listing, and the other directories that still copy from one another. A nickname on one listing, an old number on another, and a street address you do not use on a third will confuse the map and the customer. We list ourselves as Riverton, WY, with one phone number, and we keep it consistent. Your related listings should follow the same rule: one real name, one real town, one real phone.

A practical owner checklist

If the phone is quiet, walk this list yourself before you spend money on ads:

  • Is your primary Google category the work you actually want to be hired for, and are your services filled in?
  • Do your photos show recent, real jobs — not a handful of old or generic pictures?
  • Have you asked recent happy customers for a review, and do you reply to the ones you have?
  • Can a visitor on a phone tap to call from the top of your website?
  • Does the mobile page load cleanly and say what you do, where you work, and how to reach you?
  • Do you have pages for your main services and for the towns you truly cover?
  • Is your name, city, and phone identical on your website, Google listing, and other directories?
